# Lindqvist — motor card, rewritten twice

The trace is the controller talking to the positioner. I copied the bytes and then spilled coffee on the legend, so this is the second copy.

Most lines are the motor clearing its throat. Start. Creep. Stop. They are loud and they are not the question. The lines that send the dish to a place are longer, and they share a family resemblance at the front. Pell says the first line is the only one that matters. The first line is a throat-clearing. He is wrong again.

The angle is finer than a degree. The old gear counted a degree in small slices and packed the count into the end of the order. One nibble says whether you are looking east or west. I will not pretend I remember which nibble tonight. The catalog was built by someone who did remember: each slot has the angle this site’s motor would actually be given, east positive.

Pell’s formula, preserved so I can mock it later: “degrees equals the last byte divided by ten.” The dish never went where that formula points.

When an order matches a slot, keep that slot’s scrap. Read the scraps in the order the dish was sent, not in the order the catalog lists the sky.

The sticker on the controller says USALS, which is a brand name. It is not a separate code.
