# Lindqvist — coax card, night of the walk-off

The positioner at Kestrel Array still speaks the old way. A whistle on the motor coax, there and then not there. That is how a dish used to be told to move, before anyone trusted a network.

I tapped the coax because the dish left the bird twice in one window. The scope file is that tap.

It is not the Ku-band downlink. It is not a voice, even when the monitor cone buzzes in sympathy. It is not the Morse tape in the drawer. That tape is me, three years ago, failing the letter R on the station callsign.

Things I wrote down so I would stop chasing them:

- Jacket print: “LNB 10.6 — do not swap with dish 2.” That is the brick’s local oscillator. It is not hiding in the samples.
- Drawer card: “pilot, same whistle as always.” The pitch is not the message. Whether the whistle is breathing is the message.
- Pell’s theory, offered twice: the bursts are Morse. I counted dots until I got angry. They are not Morse. Pell will be wrong again before breakfast.
- One page of the service manual survived. It says each little decision takes the same breath, and that a long lit breath and a short lit breath are different decisions. It does not say which decision is which. The page ends with “odd, like the old gear,” which I think is the manual being superstitious about the last tick.

If I am right, the motor was only bragging in ordinary letters. Nobody wrapped those letters in a second code.
