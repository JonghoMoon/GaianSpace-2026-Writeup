# Lindqvist — how an order is supposed to look

The positioner ignores a bare shout. An order is a small note: who is speaking, what they want, the angle, a nonce so the note cannot be replayed, and a seal. The console on the bench speaks that language. Read it. The log is the same language, already sealed by someone the dish trusted.

The seal is not a password from the drawer. It is derived from the station and from the orbital day of this pass. The station is written on the door. The day is painted on the wall as 26278. The fraction after the dot is the part someone scratched off. I do not know how many digits the scratch ate. The console will, once you stop handing it a shrug.

Parking at home is the order the bridge actually wants: the dish brought back to rest. Any other sealed order just proves you can talk. Home is the one that answers with the token.

There is also a sealed copy of that token sitting next to the console, locked with the same secret. You can ask the console, or you can open the copy. Pell’s plan, “say the station name three times,” opens neither.

The fraction is not in this folder. Do not trust a comment that claims it is.
