County extension course - "Satellites for the farm" - my notes (B.C.)
=====================================================================

Day 1 - channels
- A satellite image is not one picture. It is a stack of channels, one per
  band of light. Side by side they look almost the same. They are not.
- Blue, green, red: what the eye sees. Near-infrared (NIR): what healthy leaves
  shout in. The NIR channel is the one that looks most different.
- SWIR (short-wave infrared): soil and dry stuff bright, water and wet leaves dark.
- "True colour" = red, green, blue. Nice for the wall. Says nothing about the crop.
- Copernicus Sentinel-2 is free. 10 m pixels. Every few days if no clouds.

Day 2 - MultiSpec lab
- Open the image, look at each channel on its own first.
- Unsupervised: Processor -> Cluster -> ISODATA. We always set it to 7 classes.
  The instructor: "the computer does not know what corn is, it only knows which
  pixels look alike in all channels at once."
- Then compare the class map with the ground truth fields. Name the classes.
- Supervised: draw training fields, Processor -> Classify, maximum likelihood
  (Gaussian). It also writes a probability file: greener = more sure.
- Fieldwork is expensive. Leave-one-out: train without a field, test on it.

Day 3 - money
- The paying agency checks area claims against Copernicus images.
  Forest, pasture, arable, built-up. If the image says pasture and you
  claimed corn, expect a letter.
- Keep screenshots of your own classification for every claim.
